US offers Audi tariff relief to keep its $4-billion Georgia plant

Audi wants its 15% tariff on EU imports cut to 7.5%; the Commerce Department is expected to settle near 10% to secure roughly 4,500 jobs.

Capital Markets Bulletin · October 30, 2026 · Source: Reuters (via Kitco)

The Trump administration has promised tariff relief to Audi to help secure the automaker's $4 billion assembly plant in Georgia, two sources briefed on the discussions told Reuters. The sources said US officials told Audi executives the government plans to offer a broader tariff relief program for automakers making significant investments in US production.

Audi has pushed Washington to cut the 15% tariff it currently pays on vehicles imported from the European Union to 7.5%. The sources said the Commerce Department was expected to agree to a rate of around 10% instead.

The plant is expected to assemble 200,000 SUVs a year and create approximately 4,500 jobs, according to the reporting. Construction is due to begin next year, with production starting in the early 2030s.

Audi has said tariffs on autos, steel, aluminum and parts cost it roughly €1.2 billion ($1.4 billion) last year. The company currently imports every vehicle it sells in the US market from factories in Europe or Mexico, which is what exposes it fully to the tariff rate rather than partially.

Audi chief executive Gernot Doellner said the Georgia investment decision followed "many months of careful planning and intense negotiations," adding, "If the American president invites you, you accept the invitation." The Commerce Department did not immediately comment, and Audi declined comment on the tariff discussions.

The reporting cites two anonymous sources for the substance of the talks, plus Doellner's own public statement and earlier reporting from the Wall Street Journal. Neither Audi nor the Commerce Department has confirmed the specific 10% figure the sources attribute to the department.
