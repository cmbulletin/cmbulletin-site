First Atlantic buys a Newfoundland property never tested for hydrogen

The explorer is paying 1,000,000 shares for 209 claims in the Bay of Islands Ophiolite Complex, where nearby ground shows historic chromite finds.

Capital Markets Bulletin · November 1, 2026 · Source: Investing News Network · TSXV:FAN, OTCQB:FANCF, FSE:P210

First Atlantic Nickel & Cobalt Corp. (TSXV: FAN) (OTCQB: FANCF) (FSE: P210) has signed an agreement, dated October 30, 2026, to acquire 100% of the North Arm Ophiolite Property on Newfoundland's west coast from two arm's-length vendors. The company will pay with 1,000,000 common shares issued at a deemed price of $0.55 each and will reimburse $51,250 in refundable security deposits the vendors had posted with the government of Newfoundland and Labrador.

The property comprises three mineral licences totalling 209 claim cells across 52.25 square kilometres on the north shore of North Arm, within the Bay of Islands Ophiolite Complex. It lies about 25 kilometres north of First Atlantic's existing Ophiolite-X project. The company says the ground has seen limited modern exploration and has never been systematically tested for geologic hydrogen or for awaruite, a naturally occurring nickel-iron-cobalt alloy.

First Atlantic points to historic chromite occurrences at nearby Blow Me Down and Springers Hill, and to active hydrogen-bearing alkaline springs at the nearby Tablelands, which the company says demonstrate ongoing serpentinization. The property itself carries no underlying royalty or option payments to a third party.

Closing is conditional on TSX Venture Exchange acceptance and is expected about five business days afterward, by January 31, 2027. The 1,000,000 payment shares will be subject to a four-month-and-one-day hold period from issuance, the standard resale restriction attached to shares issued outside a prospectus offering.

The company also granted 950,000 incentive stock options to directors and to an investor-relations consultant, exercisable at $0.55 per share for five years. The consultant's options vest quarterly over twelve months; the release does not state vesting terms for the options granted to directors.

First Atlantic's existing project in the region, Pipestone XL, spans a 30-kilometre stretch of ophiolite complex and includes awaruite deposits the company says carry about 77% nickel content along with secondary chromium mineralization, according to the company's own description of its business.
