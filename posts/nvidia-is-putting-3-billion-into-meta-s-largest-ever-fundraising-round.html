Nvidia is putting $3 billion into Meta's largest-ever fundraising round

The chipmaker's stake deepens a partnership on Meta's Muse AI assistant, built on Nvidia hardware, as Meta boosts its planned offering to $97.5 billion.

October 27, 2026Reuters (via KSL.com)

Nvidia is investing $3 billion in Meta Platforms' equity offering, which Meta increased to $97.5 billion. Reuters reported the enlarged raise is the largest fundraising in Meta's history. Meta is raising the money as it accelerates investment in AI infrastructure, Reuters reported.

The deal deepens an existing partnership on Muse, Meta's personal AI assistant, internally known as Hatch, which runs on Nvidia infrastructure. Muse can access a user's email, calendar, payments, health, shopping and smart-home applications, with users able to revoke that access at any time, according to the reporting.

The companies are also exploring joint work on Nvidia's OpenShell framework and a monitoring tool called Sentry, which is intended to track AI agent behaviour, Reuters reported.

Chief executive Mark Zuckerberg has described Muse as central to delivering what he calls "personal superintelligence" to Meta's users; the system is modelled on the open-source AI agent OpenClaw, according to the reporting.

On the venture boards, the news coincided with trading in the artificial-intelligence theme, a group of 29 listed names of which 19 traded today. The group's median move was 0.00%, against +0.53% for the S&P/TSX Venture Composite — a spread of 0.53 percentage points below the broader junior index.

Three of the larger movers were Sparc AI Inc. (CSE: SPAI), which rose 36.90% to CAD2.3; Yocale.ai Inc. (CSE: YAI), which rose 1.33% to CAD0.76; and Railtown AI Technologies Inc. (CSE: RAIL), which was unchanged at CAD0.28. Each is in the theme because its registered name contains "ai".

Nothing ties these moves to the Nvidia-Meta announcement; the two events fall on the same trading day, and the venture board's theme, taken as a whole, underperformed the broader Venture Composite rather than outperforming it.
