US and Canada edge toward a deal to ease steel and auto tariffs

Talks center on Section 232 duties of up to 50% on steel and 25% on autos, with Canadian sources saying a deal before the November 3 elections is possible but would likely be narrower in scope.

October 27, 2026Reuters (via Investing.com)

The United States and Canada are working on a deal to ease U.S. tariffs on Canadian steel and autos, Reuters reported, citing sources briefed on the talks. The negotiations aim to repair relations that soured after broader trade talks broke down in August, and focus on Section 232 duties — a US trade-law provision under which Washington has imposed tariffs of up to 50% on Canadian steel and 25% on Canadian autos.

Trump has said he intends to raise the Canadian steel tariff to 50% from its current level starting January 1, 2027, absent a deal. He predicted at a political rally on Sunday that the trade dispute would be resolved "soon," without giving specifics, Reuters reported.

Because Section 232 tariffs are administered by the US Commerce Department, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is at the center of the talks rather than the Office of the US Trade Representative, and Reuters reported he has been in regular contact with Canada's Dominic LeBlanc, the minister handling the file for Ottawa.

A spokesperson for LeBlanc described the discussions as "constructive dialogue" on "a range of priorities." Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said there had been a "slight intensification" in talks, adding that "to the extent any progress has been made, it's because the US is coming closer to our position." One Canadian source briefed on the negotiations told Reuters that a deal was possible before the November 3 US midterm elections. A separate Canadian industry source told Reuters that any deal was likely to be "a narrowed deal" if it happens.

Trump signed an order on Friday removing the 50% tariff on some Canadian agricultural and forestry products, Reuters reported.
