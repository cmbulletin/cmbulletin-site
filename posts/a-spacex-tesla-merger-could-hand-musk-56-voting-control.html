A SpaceX-Tesla merger could hand Musk 56% voting control

Barclays estimates the combination would give Musk roughly 37% ownership of the combined company, without triggering the $1-trillion Tesla pay package shareholders approved.

Capital Markets Bulletin · November 3, 2026 · Source: Reuters (via Investing.com)

A merger between SpaceX and Tesla could hand Elon Musk substantially more control over the combined company than the $1-trillion pay package Tesla shareholders approved last year, Reuters reported. Barclays analysts estimate that in a merger, Musk would own roughly 37% of the combined entity but hold about 56% of its voting power, a figure well above the roughly 29% voting control the Tesla package alone would eventually give him if every milestone is met.

The difference comes from how the two pay structures are built. Tesla's package requires Musk to hit a series of market-capitalization and operational milestones before any additional stock, and the voting power that comes with it, is released. SpaceX's own compensation plan, approved separately, gives Musk 85% voting control from the outset, with no operational conditions attached, because of a special class of shares that carries ten votes each.

SpaceX's stock-based incentives are themselves tied to ambitious targets — Mars colonization, data-center expansion, and further market-capitalization goals — that could be worth as much as $719 billion at the highest tier, according to the reporting. Under a merger scenario, any additional Tesla-linked award would not trigger until the combined company's market capitalization reached $2 trillion, versus Tesla's current standalone valuation of about $1.6 trillion; reaching that threshold would issue Musk $20 billion in shares.

Governance observers quoted in the reporting were skeptical that the pay structure itself was the real story. Corporate governance expert Charles Elson called the package "a distraction from the real story, which is complete control." Accounting professor Matthew Bloomfield described the SpaceX plan as "just a marketing document," saying Musk includes ambitious targets to "get a bunch of people hyped."

SpaceX completed its initial public offering in the summer of 2026, and the merger itself remains speculative — there is no confirmed transaction, only analysis of what one could look like under the two companies' existing pay structures. The $1-trillion Tesla package approved by shareholders last year was structured around milestones Musk had not yet met at the time of the reporting.
