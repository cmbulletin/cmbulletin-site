Washington's China chip curbs put SK Hynix's $45-billion New York plant in doubt

SK Hynix wants to fund the Marcy, New York NAND plants with profits from an expanded China plant Commerce officials have not approved.

Capital Markets Bulletin · November 4, 2026 · Source: Reuters via Investing.com · CSE:NIOB, TSXV:NMI, CSE:QESS, CSE:APXC, TSXV:TUNG

SK Hynix's American subsidiary, Solidigm, plans to invest approximately $45 billion to build two NAND flash memory plants in Marcy, New York, with the long-term investment potentially reaching $100 billion, according to a Reuters report. NAND is a type of flash memory used to store data persistently in devices including data centres. The company intends to fund the U.S. project using profits generated by expanding its existing chipmaking plant in Dalian, China.

Solidigm has requested U.S. export licences for American-made semiconductor manufacturing equipment needed to expand the Dalian facility, but the Commerce Department has not yet approved them, Reuters reported. Solidigm executives have met with Commerce Department officials nearly 10 times since late August, most recently last week. Commerce Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler maintained in that meeting that additional American chipmaking tools for capacity expansion or facility upgrades at Dalian cannot be permitted, according to the report.

The report said the Commerce Department has not formally rejected a licence for equipment destined for empty cleanroom space at Dalian, and that separate negotiations are continuing over 2027 equipment-maintenance licences. Leland Miller of the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission was quoted saying, "Ultimately, none of this is going to go forward without a green light from the White House."
