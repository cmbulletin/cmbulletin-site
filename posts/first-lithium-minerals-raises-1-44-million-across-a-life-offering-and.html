First Lithium Minerals raises $1.44 million across a LIFE offering and placement

The financing combined a Listed Issuer Financing Exemption offering with a private placement, pricing units from 11 to 15 cents to fund exploration in Chile and Ontario.

Capital Markets Bulletin · October 30, 2026 · Source: Newsfile Corp. (via finanznachrichten.de) · CSE:FLM, OTC:FLMCF, FSE:X28

First Lithium Minerals Corp. (CSE: FLM, OTC Pink: FLMCF, FSE: X28) explores for lithium and other alkali metals at the Salar de Ascotan in northern Chile, and for gold and critical metals at the Lidstone project in northwestern Ontario. The company said on October 29 that it has closed the second tranche of its Listed Issuer Financing Exemption, or LIFE, offering together with a concurrent private placement, for combined gross proceeds of $1,442,144.

The LIFE tranche raised $597,345 through 2,466,773 non-flow-through units at $0.11 per unit and 2,173,333 flow-through units at $0.15 per unit. A Listed Issuer Financing Exemption lets a company already listed on an exchange sell securities to the public without a prospectus, provided it meets disclosure conditions set by securities regulators.

The concurrent private placement raised a further $844,840 through 7,680,363 units at $0.11 per unit. Every unit across both offerings — LIFE and private placement alike — carries one-half of a common share purchase warrant, with a whole warrant exercisable at $0.18 per share for three years from closing.

The private placement units are subject to the standard statutory hold period of four months and one day from closing. The LIFE Offering units, consistent with the exemption's design, carry no such hold and are freely tradeable once issued.

First Lithium paid cash finder's fees of $23,908 plus 220,542 finder's warrants at $0.11 in connection with the LIFE tranche, and $33,387 in cash plus 337,702 finder's warrants in connection with the private placement.

The company said net proceeds will go toward exploration activities at its Chile and Ontario projects, along with general corporate purposes and working capital. The release, filed through Newsfile Corp. from Toronto, did not break out how the proceeds will be divided between the two projects.
