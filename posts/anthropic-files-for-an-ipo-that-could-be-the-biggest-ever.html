Anthropic files for an IPO that could be the biggest ever

The AI company could raise up to $100 billion at a valuation of $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion, even as its chief executive warns of a risk to humanity from unchecked AI.

Capital Markets Bulletin · October 27, 2026 · Source: Associated Press (via ClickOrlando) · NASDAQ:ANT

Anthropic has filed papers for an initial public offering that could be the largest in history, the Associated Press reported. The filing sets up a Wall Street debut as soon as next month, with the company potentially raising as much as $100 billion at a valuation of $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion — topping the $86 billion raised by SpaceX's IPO in June, previously the record.

Anthropic plans to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT. The company was founded in 2021 by a group of former OpenAI employees, including siblings Dario Amodei, its chief executive, and Daniela Amodei, its president. It reported revenue of more than $16 billion for the first half of 2026, fourteen times the prior year's figure, according to the AP.

Anthropic also reported a net loss of $80.7 billion for the period, which includes a one-time accounting charge of $72.4 billion. The company has not yet reported a profit. Its seven founders are each expected to hold about 1.4% of the company, a stake that could be worth as much as $28 billion apiece at the top of the expected valuation range, the AP reported.

The founders will retain 50.1% voting control through a special class of shares, and the company has established a Long-Term Benefit Trust of independent directors with the power to appoint board members, structured to keep financial interests from overriding public-safety considerations, according to the filing as reported by the AP.

Dario Amodei has separately argued, in a post on his website, for slowing development of the most advanced AI models, warning that without an industry slowdown, AI could let loose "a swarm of agents" that could "take over the entire internet" within six to 12 months, the AP reported.
