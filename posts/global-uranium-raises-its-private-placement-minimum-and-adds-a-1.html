Global Uranium raises its private placement minimum and adds a $1 million loan

The Calgary-based explorer increased the minimum subscription to $800,000 and secured a separate credit facility to fund its Saskatchewan and Wyoming uranium projects.

Capital Markets Bulletin · November 4, 2026 · Source: GlobeNewswire (via Manila Times) · CSE:GURN, OTCQB:GURFF

Global Uranium Corp. (CSE: GURN; OTCQB: GURFF) filed an amended and restated offering document on November 3, 2026 for its non-brokered private placement under the listed issuer financing exemption (LIFE). The minimum subscription was increased to 3,636,364 units for gross proceeds of $800,000.08; the maximum offering remains 4,239,711 units for $932,736.42, both at an unchanged price of $0.22 per unit.

Each unit comprises one common share and one warrant exercisable at $0.30 per share. The warrant does not become exercisable until 61 days after closing and expires 24 months after closing. Closing is expected on or about November 6, 2026, subject to regulatory approvals including those of the Canadian Securities Exchange.

The release said the securities are expected to be immediately freely tradable under applicable Canadian securities laws, subject to the restrictions that apply to control persons, and that the offering is available to purchasers in Canada other than Quebec and New Brunswick.

Separately, the Calgary-based uranium explorer entered a committed, unsecured, non-revolving loan facility of up to $1,000,000 with an arm's-length lender. Advances can be requested on five business days' notice until October 30, 2028, each carrying 12% simple annual interest and due for repayment 24 months after it is drawn, with all amounts owed no later than November 3, 2030. No funds had been drawn as of the announcement.

The facility carries no demand-repayment clause and allows prepayment without penalty, the company said. Proceeds from both the placement and the facility are earmarked for exploration expenditures and option payments on the company's Astro uranium project in Saskatchewan and its Wyoming projects in the Great Divide Basin, Gas Hills and Copper Mountain districts, as well as corporate liabilities, administration and working capital.

The release does not name the arm's-length lender, and does not name an agent for the private placement.
