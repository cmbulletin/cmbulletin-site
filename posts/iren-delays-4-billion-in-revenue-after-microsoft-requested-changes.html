IREN delays $4 billion in revenue after Microsoft requested changes

IREN said the delayed revenue remains fully contracted and funded, even as its shares stayed down 55% over the past month in a broader slide across AI data-centre stocks.

Capital Markets Bulletin · November 6, 2026 · Source: CNBC · NASDAQ:IREN, CSE:AIML, CSE:RAIL, CSE:AIDR, TSXV:CTRL, CSE:SPAI

IREN Limited — described by CNBC as a "neocloud" — said Thursday that more than $4 billion of contracted annualized recurring revenue it had expected to reach by the end of 2026 will now arrive in January, and potentially as late as February. The company said the shift followed "a customer-requested change and supplier timing." On the earnings call, co-CEO Daniel Roberts identified the customer making the request as Microsoft, CNBC reported.

Roberts told CNBC that Microsoft had asked IREN to "reconfigure and upgrade some of the network infrastructure," and that the purpose was to let 200 megawatts of capacity serve "these latest-generation frontier models." Despite the later timing, the company said the more-than-$4-billion target remains "fully contracted and funded." IREN's shares rose in extended trading immediately after the results were released, CNBC reported, even after weeks of declines.

The report followed a rough stretch across AI infrastructure stocks: IREN's own shares had fallen 26% after Tuesday's U.S. midterm elections and were down 55% over the past month, CNBC reported, as the Nasdaq suffered its worst trading day since April 2025. Two other AI-infrastructure companies have fared similarly: CoreWeave has dropped 60% over the past month, and Nebius has declined 48% over the same period, both ahead of their own earnings reports this week.

Roberts told CNBC that "capital markets have become more selective in the last few weeks," adding that the market's questions had moved from "how big the contract is to whether it can be delivered, financed, and run at a profit." He said "there is a disconnect between what we're seeing in public market and what we're seeing at the customer level." IREN holds contracts with Nvidia, Microsoft and 13 other customers, CNBC reported.

Quarterly revenue rose 66% sequentially to $228 million, beating the $221 million average analyst estimate compiled by LSEG, CNBC reported. AI Cloud revenue more than doubled to $200 million, while IREN's net loss narrowed to $263 million from $684 million in the prior quarter. The company's bitcoin mining business, its original line of business before the shift to AI, supplied the remainder of quarterly revenue.

Revenue backlog grew 22% to $20.3 billion, with roughly two-thirds of it held by investment-grade counterparties, the company said. IREN also said it plans $15 billion of capital expenditure over the rest of its fiscal year, with $9 billion already covered by cash and committed financing and a further $6 billion in "target" financing "subject to market conditions." One customer has agreed to finance 90% of the GPU capital expenditure, Roberts said, and roughly a third of planned data-centre construction is being funded through customer prepayments for capacity.

To verify independently

  • IREN's statement that the more-than-$4-billion revenue target remains "fully contracted and funded" despite the delay rests on the company's own characterization and is not independently verified.
  • The identification of Microsoft as the customer requesting the design change comes from Daniel Roberts' comments on the earnings call, as reported by CNBC, rather than from a written release naming the customer.
