Argentum AI is negotiating $5 billion in debt to buy more AI chips

The Chicago-based neocloud, which already has $12 billion in contracted revenue, wants the financing to lease chips to customers without investment-grade credit.

Capital Markets Bulletin · October 28, 2026 · Source: Reuters (via Investing.com)

Argentum AI, a Chicago-based cloud computing company backed by Super Micro, is in talks with a major private credit firm for a $5 billion financing deal, Reuters reported on Tuesday, citing two people familiar with the matter. The debt would fund additional AI chips for a data centre Argentum is building in Poland with DL Invest Group.

The financing is intended for chips leased to customers that do not carry investment-grade credit ratings, according to the sources. Argentum was founded in 2025 by chief executive Andrew Sobko and describes itself as an independent, institutional-grade AI cloud platform focused on deploying large-scale GPU infrastructure for enterprise AI.

Argentum has more than $12 billion in contracted revenue, Reuters reported. In May, the company signed roughly a $2.5 billion agreement with cloud-gaming firm Boosteroid and DL Invest Group for a 300-megawatt data centre facility.

The talks come a day after SpaceX secured $40 billion in debt financing for chips from an affiliate of Apollo Global Management, as financing has tightened for "neoclouds" — firms that rent out AI computing power. Elon Musk said "debt markets have almost entirely shut off access to money for companies without substantial revenue independent of AI data centers," and predicted significant consolidation among the sector's more than 200 competitors.
