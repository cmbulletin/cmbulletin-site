A proxy adviser is telling H&R REIT investors to reject a $3.4-billion buyout

ISS cited concerns about the sale process and an insider-linked side deal ahead of the November 13 unitholder vote on the GO Residential takeover.

Capital Markets Bulletin · October 28, 2026 · Source: Globe and Mail — Business

Proxy adviser Institutional Shareholder Services is recommending that unitholders of H&R Real Estate Investment Trust vote against the trust's proposed $3.4-billion acquisition by GO Residential Real Estate Investment Trust, a deal announced in August. Under its terms, H&R unitholders would receive $4.28 in cash plus 0.5688 of a GO unit for every H&R unit they hold. The vote is scheduled for November 13.

ISS's recommendation cited concerns over the sale process, conflicts of interest, the logic of the deal and whether disinterested unitholders are capturing fair value relative to insiders. The adviser pointed to a related transaction in which a company controlled by H&R chief executive Tom Hofstedter would acquire $1.98 billion of properties, at book value, for $1.56 billion — different consideration than other unitholders are receiving in the main transaction.

Several institutional investors have already said publicly they intend to vote against the deal, including RBC Global Asset Management, Mill Pond Capital and Sandpiper Group. Rival proxy adviser Glass Lewis is expected to publish its own recommendation in the coming week. GO Residential's unit price has declined since the deal was announced, reducing the value of the consideration H&R unitholders would receive. H&R faces a $102-million termination fee if the transaction does not proceed.

GO Residential Real Estate Investment Trust owns luxury apartments in New York. As part of the transaction, GO would issue 134.2 million of its own units to help fund the acquisition of $1.8 billion of residential properties.
