AMD takes a $1.07 billion charge tied to its OpenAI and Meta chip deals

The non-cash charge reflects warrants that let the two AI customers buy up to roughly 10% of the chipmaker.

Capital Markets Bulletin · November 4, 2026 · Source: Reuters (via WHBL)

Advanced Micro Devices recorded a $1.07 billion non-cash charge, equal to 66 cents a share, in the third quarter ended September 26. The charge relates to warrants the chipmaker has issued to OpenAI and Meta Platforms as part of multibillion-dollar agreements to supply them with AI chips.

Under a February agreement, AMD agreed to sell Meta up to $60 billion of AI chips over five years. Meta received warrants allowing it to buy as much as 10% of AMD if certain shipment milestones are met.

AMD shares rose 8% in extended trading after the results, and the company forecast 2027 growth faster than previously expected. Bob O'Donnell, chief analyst at TECHnalysis Research, said of the broader trend of chipmakers issuing customer warrants: "While I do believe incentives played a role in some of their sales, I believe the bigger issue is that there is pent-up demand for a serious alternative to Nvidia."

AMD is not alone in using warrants to secure large AI customers. Cerebras has granted OpenAI warrants against a $20 billion commitment, Marvell issued Google warrants for a potential $12.2 billion stake, and Qualcomm gave Amazon warrants as part of a $60 billion deal, according to the report.
