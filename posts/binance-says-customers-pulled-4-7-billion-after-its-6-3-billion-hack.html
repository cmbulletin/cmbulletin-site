Binance says customers pulled $4.7 billion after its $6.3 billion hack

The exchange reopened withdrawals after a 48-hour freeze following the theft, which the FBI has linked to the North Korean hacking group TraderTraitor.

Capital Markets Bulletin · November 2, 2026 · Source: Reuters (via BNN Bloomberg) · TSXV:NDA, TSXV:DMGI

Binance said Monday that customers had withdrawn a net $4.72 billion from the exchange since it reopened withdrawals on Sunday morning. The company had frozen withdrawals for roughly 48 hours after disclosing a $6.3 billion theft, which it has called potentially the largest financial heist in history. The exchange reported $2.7 billion of inflows after reopening, and said withdrawal volumes had since declined and stabilized.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation has attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, a hacking group the agency links to North Korea. Binance co-chief executive Yi He said the exchange's "top priority right now is to ensure the environment is secure, after which users can withdraw their funds." Binance said it is covering customer losses with company funds, having already raised $6 billion from its corporate treasury, an insurance fund and reserves set aside for security breaches.

The stablecoin issuer Circle said it had frozen more than $1.1 billion in USDC tied to the theft. Bitcoin, which had fallen to a six-week low after the hack became public, had recovered to around $78,000 by Monday.

Spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded $643 million of outflows the Friday before Binance reopened withdrawals, and more than $1.3 billion in leveraged crypto positions were liquidated in the aftermath of the hack, according to the reporting. North Korea-linked hackers have stolen a combined $7.4 billion from crypto platforms in 2026.
