Midterm results lift hopes for an end to 50% aluminum tariffs

Canadian producers have already rerouted roughly 800,000 tonnes of shipments to Europe as Washington's 50 per cent tariff reshapes trade flows.

Capital Markets Bulletin · November 4, 2026 · Source: Globe and Mail — Business

Canada's aluminum industry is watching the outcome of the U.S. midterm elections for a sign that Washington might ease the 50 per cent tariff it has placed on Canadian aluminum exports. Jean Simard, chief executive of the Aluminum Association of Canada, said he is 'far more optimistic than I've ever been, because the signal is overwhelmingly clearer than I thought it would be.'

The 50 per cent rate has been in place since June 4, 2025, when Washington doubled an earlier 25 per cent tariff that took effect in March 2025. Tariffs on Canadian aluminum go back further still: a 10 per cent duty was first imposed in 2018. Three producers account for most of the Canadian industry's primary output: Rio Tinto, Alcoa and Aluminerie Alouette.

Faced with the tariff, Canadian producers have redirected shipments toward Europe. Exports there have totalled roughly 800,000 tonnes since March 2025, against about 100,000 tonnes over a comparable period under normal market conditions. Canada produces about 3.3 million tonnes of aluminum annually and has historically sent about 90 per cent of it to the United States.

Andreas Schotter of Western University cautioned against reading the midterm results as a clean signal that U.S. trade policy will soften, saying 'even on the Democratic side, there's a strong "America First" notion.' The Canadian Federation of Independent Business has reported that 87 per cent of Canadian small businesses want a tariff agreement reached as quickly as possible.

Nothing in this report ties the midterm results to any specific change in U.S. trade policy; Simard's optimism is his own reading of the political signal, not a description of an announced decision.
