Nasdaq futures jump 1.4% as chipmakers rebound from a $3 trillion rout

Nvidia, AMD and Micron rose in premarket trading a day after a rout tied to AI-regulation fears from Democrats' midterm win wiped nearly $3 trillion off the S&P 500.

Nasdaq 100 E-minis rose 378.5 points, or 1.41%, by 6:24 a.m. ET Friday, while S&P 500 E-minis added 21.75 points, or 0.29%, and Dow E-minis fell 471 points, or 0.91%, Reuters reported. The moves followed Thursday's rout, in which the Nasdaq Composite posted its steepest one-day decline in 19 months and erased nearly $3 trillion from S&P 500 market value. Read the full story

The rout followed Democrats' midterm election victory, including wins in several data-center states, which stoked fears that tougher AI regulation could slow data-center construction, Reuters reported. Jane Gibbons, an equity strategist at Jefferies, called the selloff 'reactionary, not a fundamental reset, driven by a collision of concerns around AI spend, policy and inflation.' IREN delays $4 billion in revenue after Microsoft requested changes

IREN said the delayed revenue remains fully contracted and funded, even as its shares stayed down 55% over the past month in a broader slide across AI data-centre stocks.

IREN Limited said Thursday that more than $4 billion of contracted annualized revenue, expected by the end of 2026, will now arrive in January and potentially February. The company said the shift followed "a customer-requested change and supplier timing"; on the earnings call, co-CEO Daniel Roberts identified the customer as Microsoft, CNBC reported.

IREN said the target remains fully contracted and funded despite the delay. Quarterly revenue rose 66% to $228 million and AI Cloud revenue more than doubled to $200 million, while net loss narrowed to $263 million from $684 million. The company also plans $15 billion more in capital spending this fiscal year.

Gold climbs to $4,063 an ounce as Democrats sweep control of Congress

Spot silver also rose to $52.32 an ounce and the US Dollar Index slipped to near 101.5 after Democrats won both the House and the Senate in the midterms.

Spot gold climbed above US$4,000 an ounce on November 4, reaching US$4,063, while spot silver rose to US$52.32 by late afternoon. The move came as Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate in the US midterm elections, leaving President Trump facing an opposition Congress for the rest of his term.

The US Dollar Index fell about 0.3% to near 101.5 and Treasury yields moved lower. Analysts quoted in the report, including Peter Schiff of Euro Pacific Asset Management and Rich Checkan of Asset Strategies International, linked the moves to dollar weakness and expectations of higher government spending under a Democratic Congress.
