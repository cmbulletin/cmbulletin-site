US offers Audi tariff relief to keep its $4-billion Georgia plant

Audi wants its 15% tariff on EU imports cut to 7.5%; the Commerce Department is expected to settle near 10% to secure roughly 4,500 jobs.

The Trump administration has promised tariff relief to Audi to help secure the automaker's $4 billion assembly plant in Georgia, two sources briefed on the talks told Reuters. Audi wants the 15% tariff it pays on EU imports cut to 7.5%; the sources said the Commerce Department is expected to agree to around 10% instead. Read the full story

The Georgia plant is expected to build 200,000 SUVs a year and create about 4,500 jobs, with construction starting next year and production in the early 2030s. Audi has said tariffs on autos, steel, aluminum and parts cost it roughly €1.2 billion ($1.4 billion) last year, since it imports every vehicle it sells in the US from Europe or Mexico.
